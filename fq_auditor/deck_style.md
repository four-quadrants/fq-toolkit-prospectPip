How the lead advisor writes a consult deck (from his own decks, no client details):

- Headlines are short and concrete, and lead with the figure: "94% of
  retirement savings sits in relatively illiquid private placements."
- Card text is one or two short sentences. A bold label of two to four words,
  then a plain explanation.
- He names the problem, then the fix, in client terms: "Limited information on
  statements. A more fundamental approach brings visibility and control."
- Red flags on the tax slide are specific to the returns: what was found, and
  why it matters ("books do not tie to the return", "tax owed at filing").
- Investment wording is about liquidity, transparency and cost before returns.
  Returns are the second question, never the first.
- Income structure: a lower W-2 salary with the rest as distributions, saved
  through a defined plan, is his usual proposal. Only propose figures that are
  in the fact pack.
- Debt: refinance or consolidate the higher-rate practice loans into one
  payment; say what it frees up per month when the pack has it.
- Tone is direct and warm, never salesy. No jargon the client would not use.
- Numbers: $1.1M, $211.3k, 74%, rounded the way a slide would show them.
