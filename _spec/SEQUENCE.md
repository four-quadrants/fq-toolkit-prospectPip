# The prospect sequence, as the firm runs it

Settled with Austin, 22 October 2026, from the 9 October pipeline map
(`FQ_Pipeline_Map.pdf`) with his corrections. The app is laid out to follow
this, not the other way round.

| # | Moment | What happens | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Initial list | First document request goes out in LaunchBay | portal open |
| 2 | Intake | Pull, download, read, review against the checklist, file | documents filed |
| 3 | Initial notes | Basic analysis: annual production, overhead per the taxes and per the P&L, call notes, the IC form | **initial notes workbook** (the real value); the Prospect Summary is built quietly beside it, for anyone who wants the visual |
| 4 | Consult offer call | Sales, made on the initial notes | go-ahead |
| 5 | Expanded list | Second, bigger request in LaunchBay | |
| 6 | Intake again | Same as step 2, on the new documents | |
| 7 | Assumptions | Built from everything that has come in: cash flow, liabilities, all assets, insurance, income and entities, overhead, fees, production | **Assumptions** |
| 8 | Due diligence | The judgement calls, and the questions still open for the prospect | reviewed plan + questions |
| 9 | Consult deck | Built from the finished plan | **deck** |

## The rule for judgement calls (step 8)

Every judgement call has to be traceable to its source document and
explicable in arithmetic to Brogan or the onboarding specialist. Not "the
model says": the figure, where it came from, and the sum that produced it.
For example:

    Ideal practice bank balance  $280,000
      = 2025 overhead $3,279,015 (2025 P&L, Total Expenses less doctor wages
        and depreciation) / 12 = $273,251, rounded up to the next $10,000

A figure that cannot be shown this way is not a judgement call yet. It becomes
a question for the ANALYST first, and only if the analyst cannot settle it
from what is on file does it go to the prospect.

## The questions for the prospect (also step 8)

Modelled on Brogan's own follow-up emails (Oyler, 7 October: written by
Brogan, proofed and sent by Chad, cc Kevin, Will and Brogan; answered in
line by the prospect on 11 October). Nearly every one of his questions comes
from something the documents show:

| His question | What detected it |
|---|---|
| Missing statements for Reef Q3 2026 and Q4 2025 | a statement series with gaps |
| Three accounts we have no statements for (x0222, x3468, x2683) | transfers to and from accounts not on file |
| A new $50,000 Amex loan in July: rate, term, payment | a new borrowing seen in the statements |
| Boat loan term, rate, start | a liability with no schedule |
| $2,928.13 a month from "America Pacific" into Moonridge | a recurring deposit nobody explained |
| CiVista loan looks like 8 years, not 9 | amortisation not matching the stated term |
| SBA portion of the building loan | a loan part with no statement |
| Two valuations; who did the second | two documents for one fact |
| How far booked out, hygiene, new patients | IC form gaps (not in documents at all) |
| Where the $113k of closing funds came from | a large outflow with no source |

So the toolkit drafts the list (each question with the finding behind it), the
analyst strikes or answers what they can, and what is left becomes the email
in Brogan's format for Chad to send. The prospect's answers come back as an
email and are filed with the documents.

## Credit reports

Recognised and filed today ("Personal - Credit Reports"), but never read.
They are the best single cross-check on liabilities: every tradeline's
creditor, balance, payment, open date and status, to set against the
statements, the debt schedule and the IC form. A loan on the credit report
and nowhere else is a question; a payment in the statements with no
tradeline is another. Oyler's arrived as five phone photos; Rebol's as five
bureau PDFs (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion for both spouses).
